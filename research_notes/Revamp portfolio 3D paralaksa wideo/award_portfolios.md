# Award-winning developer/creative portfolios 2024-2026 (minimalism + 3D + parallax + video)

Research date: 2026-10-24. About 18 search/fetch calls in total. Award data comes mainly from Awwwards' own listing pages, which were fetched directly. Some sources (tympanus.net/codrops articles) returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so Codrops details come from search snippets only and are marked that way. None of the live site URLs were opened to confirm they are up; they are the URLs that Awwwards and the articles list. Treat "online" status as unverified.

## Which 2024-2026 SOTD/developer portfolios best balance minimalism + 3D + parallax + video? Concrete URLs.

### Takeaway
Recent Awwwards portfolio winners are almost all strict two-colour designs: near-black plus white, or black plus one accent. They put all of their "wow" into one motion system, such as page transitions with video, a WebGL scene, or scroll-driven type, and they win the Developer Award on Animations/Transitions scores rather than on accessibility. Few individual developer portfolios carry a full real-time 3D model. The ones that do (Bruno Simon, Jordan Breton, Minh Pham, Cathy Dolle, Jesper Landberg) either make 3D the whole concept or use it as a frame around otherwise plain content.

## What recurring patterns do these winners share (hero composition, nav, type, case-study layout, loaders, cursor, page transitions)?

### Takeaway
The recurring pattern is a two-colour palette, a typography-led hero, a preloader or loading transition, image/video hover effects in the project list, and seamless page transitions, very often carrying video. Developer Awards are won on Animations/Transitions (8.4-9.0) while Accessibility stays the weakest sub-score (6.2-7.0).

### Cited Findings
- Palettes of verified winners: Landberg #000/#fff; Mangham #121212/#fff; Gradogna #0D0D0D/#fff; Lazarieva #101010/#F7F7F7; Yakushev #000; Wodniack #f40c3f/#160000. - [Landberg](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4), [Mangham](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/elliott-mangham), [Gradogna](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/gianluca-gradogna-portfolio), [Lazarieva](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/olha-lazarieva), [Yakushev](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/artiom-yakushev), [Wodniack](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/aw-portfolio)
- Highlighted elements recur across winners: loading transition, page transition, hover image effect, clip animation (Gradogna); preloader, scroll-to-reveal, video modal (Mangham); 404 page, About, Gallery (Lazarieva, Yakushev). - same pages as above
- Dev Award sub-scores: Animations/Transitions is the top sub-score (Landberg 9.00, Wodniack 8.40) and Accessibility is the lowest (Wodniack 6.20, Gradogna 6.80, Landberg 7.00). - [Landberg](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4), [Wodniack](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/aw-portfolio), [Gradogna](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/gianluca-gradogna-portfolio)
- Juror view: the three pillars are art direction, directed motion and performance. Sites are judged on real devices against roughly 60fps on mid-range hardware. A reduced-motion fallback is recommended, and "hard cuts / fades" count as weak transitions. By-Kin is praised as "a masterclass in restraint" (weighted smooth scroll, editorial type, transitions that "blend into one continuous surface"). This comes from a self-interested juror-author. - [hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Mat Voyce (matvoyce.tv; Awwwards SOTD, GSAP SOTY 2025 nominee): letters stretch, snap and recombine on scroll, "tuned so reading isn't blocked". - [hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Stack trends: GSAP is near-universal. Three.js is paired with Nuxt (Landberg) or React/Next (Lazarieva, Jean-Elie). Webflow also wins (Yakushev). Vite is used for lighter builds (Mangham). - pages above; [Codrops Jean-Elie](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/11/27/letting-the-creative-process-shape-a-webgl-portfolio/)

### Inferences
- The current portfolio already has a dark base, `prefers-reduced-motion` handling and in-viewport gating, which matches what jurors reward. The gap against winners is mostly the multi-colour template look and the number of separate effects, compared with one cohesive motion language.
- Cursor: none of the fetched winner pages highlights a custom cursor as an element. Its absence from "highlighted elements" is weak evidence that it is no longer a differentiator.

### Gaps
- Nav patterns (fixed minimal top bar vs. menu overlay) could not be confirmed from the fetched pages. Gradogna lists "Menu" as a highlighted element, but no description was given.

## Which sites use a 3D model of a physical object (device, board, product) driven by scroll, and how?

### Takeaway
Scroll-driven 3D models of physical products appear mostly on product and brand sites by studios (Lusion's Oryzo, Immersive Garden's Cartier, Unseen's Hubtown), not on personal portfolios. The technique is an inertial, Z-axis "depth" scroll that moves the camera past or into a single hero object, or a set of scrollable 3D "alcoves" with one object each.

### Cited Findings
- **Oryzo (Lusion)** - oryzo.ai - Awwwards **Site of the Month Apr 2026** + Developer Award: an "inertial 3D product render with Z-axis depth scroll", in vanilla Three.js. - [Utsubo: Best Three.js websites 2026](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)
- **Cartier Watches & Wonders 2026 (Immersive Garden)**: "six scrollable 3D alcoves, one per watch". Stack: Three.js, GLSL, GSAP, **Lenis**, Web Audio. Awwwards SOTD. - [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)
- **Hubtown (Unseen Studio)** - Awwwards SOTD Jun 2026: a "3D monolith hero with mouse-reveal interaction" (Three.js, GSAP). - [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)
- **IVRESS (Utsubo)** - FWA Site of the Month May 2026: Three.js **WebGPURenderer with WebGL fallback**, TSL shaders. - [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026) (the publisher built this site)
- In the Utsubo list, "No listed site is an individual's portfolio". - [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)
- Among portfolios: Bruno Simon's car is a physics-driven object, controlled by keyboard, touch or gamepad rather than by scroll. - [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)
- A non-award GitHub project, geetanshgoyal.in, is a Three.js room whose **CRT runs "RetroOS"**. It is a portfolio-on-a-device concept with no award confirmed. - [GitHub geetxnshgoyal/profile](https://github.com/geetxnshgoyal/profile) (snippet)

### Inferences
- For this owner, an obvious fit is a single scroll-driven model of a physical engineering object, such as an FPGA board, a PCB, or the radio-telescope dish/feed, done in the Oryzo pattern: one object, camera dolly on scroll, everything else plain HTML. It sits inside a mostly static page and stays within the "subtle" constraint. This is an inference; no award-winning portfolio doing exactly this was found.
- If using Lenis (already in the repo via `js/animation.js`), Cartier/Immersive Garden shows Lenis + GSAP + Three.js as an award-tier combination.

### Gaps
- No verified individual developer portfolio 2024-2026 was found that drives a hardware/device model by scroll.

## Any electronics / hardware / space-themed award sites worth borrowing a single idea from?

### Takeaway
No 2024-2026 Awwwards/FWA-awarded portfolio by an electronics or embedded engineer was found, and no SOTD with PCB or oscilloscope motifs either. The closest tasteful technical-motif winners are retro/"OS" portfolios (Wodniack's retro minimal SOTD, Chus Margallo's retro-OS HM). This makes an electronics-themed portfolio a relatively unoccupied niche.

### Cited Findings

- The closest "technical" portfolio found in the Awwwards Technology category is "Harikrishnan R - Portfolio", Honorable Mention Aug 2025 (snippet; details not retrieved). - [Awwwards Technology](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/technology/?page=25&mobile=1)

- **Rafael de las Heras, "Creative Developer Portfolio"**: Awwwards HM 17 Aug 2025, tagged Retro. - [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/creative-developer-portfolio-2) (snippet)
- **Wodniack** (SOTD Dec 2024): tags Retro + Minimal + Experimental, in a strict two-colour red/black palette. - [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/aw-portfolio)

### Inferences
- Ideas worth borrowing, each taken once:
  - Wodniack's discipline of one accent colour plus near-black. Applied here, the oscilloscope trace colour would become the single accent.
  - Chus Margallo's CRT/noise as a *material* rather than as decoration. For example, the oscilloscope separator could get a phosphor-glow shader.
  - The Oryzo-style single-object depth scroll, applied to the radio-telescope dish (which the site removed earlier for being too much). It would come back as one scroll-driven 3D model rather than a flat canvas effect.
- The existing Fourier-series oscilloscope separator is already a more specific and more tasteful technical motif than anything found among recent winners. It is worth keeping as the signature.

### Gaps
- Space and telescope portfolio winners were not found. The search budget was exhausted before a dedicated space/astronomy query on FWA or CSSDA.

## What patterns are now considered cliché/overused in 2025-2026 by jurors and designers?

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin and mostly from agency blogs. What there is points at aggressive parallax, scroll-jacking that hurts INP, autoplay video with sound, glassmorphism on body text, bento-grid feature sections and generic stock imagery. Juror-adjacent commentary instead stresses that a "polished template" without a point of view loses to restraint.

### Cited Findings
- "Aggressive parallax" is listed as dated, while subtle scroll effects remain fine. Autoplay video with sound is "widely rejected". Generic stock photos are fading in favour of real photos. - [Tiny Frog: 2026 trends](https://tinyfrog.com/web-design-trends)
- Scroll-jacking makes frames compete with input responsiveness. The source cites "roughly 43% of sites miss the INP threshold" (vendor source, treat with caution). - [Jacob Tyler blog](https://www.jacobtyler.com/blog/tag/page-speed/)
- Bento grids are "becoming the default pattern for feature sections". Glass works only for sticky headers and should not carry long-form text. - [The Plus Addons: web design trends 2026](https://theplusaddons.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026/)
- Custom cursors were framed as a 2024 "gimmick" trend reminiscent of the 90s. This is dated evidence. - [Vistaprint 2024 trends](https://www.vistaprint.ca/hub/web-design-trends-2024/)
- Juror-author: "polished template" vs "a specific point of view"; "hard cuts / fades" are weak. - [hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- Course-style "Awwwards-winning 3D developer portfolio" tutorials (React + Three.js + GSAP) are widespread on YouTube and GitHub, which implies those layouts are now commodity. - [Class Central / JavaScript Mastery](https://www.classcentral.com/course/youtube-build-and-deploy-an-awwwards-winning-3d-web-developer-portfolio-with-react-three-js-gsap-444175); [GitHub topic awwwards](https://github.com/topics/awwwards)

### Inferences
- Likely clichés to avoid (inferred, not juror-confirmed): the floating-laptop / "3D room with desk and monitor" hero, which the tutorials and R3F room portfolios have commoditised; a blob or custom-cursor follower; "Hi, I'm X, creative developer" giant-type hero with marquee; glass cards. The user already rejected glassmorphism, which is consistent with this.

### Gaps
- No primary juror statement (for example an Awwwards jury interview) explicitly naming 2025-2026 clichés was found. Preloaders and smooth scroll were not called clichés by any source found; the winners above still use them (Mangham preloader, By-Kin weighted smooth scroll).
