# Legal, compliance and organisational consequences of using AI (incl. AI coding agents) in companies - EU and Poland (status: October 2026)

Note on sources: items marked "(from background knowledge, verify)" were not re-fetched during this research session; everything else was checked against the cited page in October 2026. Many timeline facts on the Digital Omnibus come from law-firm/consultancy briefings that agree with each other; one (nhigovernance) states it verified the number on EUR-Lex.

## 1. EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) - deployers vs providers, Art. 4, Art. 5, GPAI, Art. 50, timeline, Digital Omnibus, Polish implementation

### Takeaway
A company that only uses AI tools (including coding agents like Copilot, Claude Code, Codex) is a "deployer"; for typical non-high-risk uses its real obligations are AI literacy (Art. 4), respecting the prohibitions (Art. 5) and some transparency duties (Art. 50) - the heavy obligations sit with providers and with high-risk systems. The Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) pushed high-risk obligations to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I), but did not move Art. 50 or GPAI dates. Poland adopted its implementing act (Ustawa z dnia 3 lipca 2026 r. o systemach sztucznej inteligencji, Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003) creating KRiBSI as the market surveillance authority.

### Cited Findings
**Roles and scope**
- Full text of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689): Art. 3(3) "provider" (develops or has developed an AI system/GPAI model and places it on the market under own name), Art. 3(4) "deployer" (uses an AI system under its authority, except personal non-professional use); Art. 25 - a deployer becomes a provider of a high-risk system if it puts its name/trademark on it, substantially modifies it or changes its intended purpose to a high-risk one (from background knowledge, verify article numbering) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Deployer obligations for high-risk systems (Art. 26): use per provider instructions, assign human oversight to competent persons, monitor operation, keep automatically generated logs (at least 6 months), and inform workers' representatives and affected workers before putting a high-risk system into use at the workplace (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Fines (Art. 99): up to EUR 35m or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices; up to EUR 15m or 3% for most other obligations; up to EUR 7.5m or 1% for supplying incorrect information (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

**AI literacy (Art. 4)**
- Original Art. 4 (applies since 2 Feb 2025): providers and deployers must take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of staff and others operating AI systems on their behalf (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Digital Omnibus softened Art. 4: providers and deployers now "support the development of AI literacy among their staff" rather than guarantee a specific level - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

**Prohibited practices (Art. 5)**
- Prohibitions (e.g. manipulative/subliminal techniques, exploitation of vulnerabilities, social scoring, emotion recognition at the workplace and in education, untargeted scraping of facial images, certain biometric categorisation) apply since 2 Feb 2025 (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)
- Omnibus adds a new ban on AI systems generating non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM, with a transitional period to 2 Dec 2026 - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)

**GPAI**
- GPAI model obligations keep their 2 Aug 2025 application date; Omnibus gives the AI Office exclusive competence over AI systems built on a GPAI model by the same provider and over AI in VLOPs/VLOSEs, plus investigation, inspection, commitment and fining tools - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- GPAI enforcement powers/penalties start 2 Aug 2026 - [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)

**Transparency (Art. 50)**
- Art. 50 obligations stay on 2 Aug 2026; systems on the market before that date get until 2 Dec 2026 for the Art. 50(2) machine-readable marking (watermarking) duty; the duty to tell people they interact with AI is unaffected - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Deployer-side Art. 50 duties: disclose deepfakes and AI-generated/manipulated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (unless human-reviewed with editorial responsibility); inform people exposed to emotion recognition/biometric categorisation (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)

**Timeline after the Omnibus**
- 2 Feb 2025: Art. 4 + Art. 5; 2 Aug 2025: GPAI + governance + penalties framework; 2 Aug 2026: general application incl. Art. 50, GPAI enforcement; 2 Dec 2027: Annex III high-risk (HR/recruitment, credit scoring, education, law enforcement, border); 2 Aug 2028: Annex I (AI in regulated products, previously 2 Aug 2027); national sandbox deadline moved to 2 Aug 2027 - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Acompli](https://acompli.ie/news/ai-omnibus-enters-into-force-july-2026/)
- Omnibus legislative path: provisional deal 6-7 May 2026, EP vote 16 June 2026, Council 29 June 2026, published as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in OJ on 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026 (6 days before the old 2 Aug 2026 high-risk deadline) - [nicfab](https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-official-journal/); [NHI Governance](https://nhigovernance.com/news/2026/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force.html); [CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-omnibus-vii-deadline-delay-20260/)
- Omnibus also narrows the "safety component" definition and extends the legal basis for processing special-category data for bias detection to all AI systems/GPAI models (strict necessity), following EDPB/EDPS Joint Opinion 1/2026 - [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Commission AI Act hub (guidelines on Art. 4 literacy, prohibited practices, GPAI Code of Practice) - [digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai)

**Poland**
- Ustawa z dnia 3 lipca 2026 r. o systemach sztucznej inteligencji, Dz.U. 2026 poz. 1003, published 27 July 2026; general entry into force 11 Aug 2026; Art. 125(4) from 28 July 2026; Art. 8-18 and chapters 3-5, 8, 9 from 28 Oct 2026; responsible minister: minister ds. informatyzacji - [ELI gov.pl](https://eli.gov.pl/eli/DU/2026/1003/ogl/pol); [ISAP PDF](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/download.xsp/WDU20260001003/T/D20261003L.pdf)
- Legislative path: government bill adopted 31 March 2026, print no. 2443 (9 April 2026), Sejm passed 11 June 2026 (421-3-18), Senate amendments 25 June, final Sejm vote 3 July, President signed 24 July 2026 - [Sejm, druk 2443](https://www.sejm.gov.pl/Sejm10.nsf/PrzebiegProc.xsp?nr=2443); [cyberdefence24](https://cyberdefence24.pl/polityka-i-prawo/polska/koniec-parlamentarnych-prac-nad-ustawa-o-sztucznej-inteligencji-czas-na-ruch-prezydenta); [rp.pl](https://www.rp.pl/prawo-w-polsce/art44606901-sejm-przyjal-ustawe-o-sztucznej-inteligencji-komisja-ds-ai-i-piaskownice-regulacyjne-dla-firm)
- Supervisory authority: Komisja Rozwoju i Bezpieczeństwa Sztucznej Inteligencji (KRiBSI) as national market surveillance authority; it can order an operator to stop using or withdraw a system posing a direct threat to life, health, safety or fundamental rights (immediately enforceable); regulatory sandboxes for firms; the 2026 version dropped the flat fines of the 2025 draft (10m PLN legal entities / 1m PLN individuals) - [Kancelaria Macura](https://www.kancelariamacura.pl/aktualnosci/ustawa-o-systemach-sztucznej-inteligencji-przyjeta-przez-sejm-kribsi-ma-stac-sie-krajowym-organem-nadzoru-nad-ai/); [PTI](https://pti.org.pl/ustawa-o-systemach-sztucznej-inteligencji-co-naprawde-uchwala-sejm-i-co-to-oznacza-w-praktyce/)
- UODO positions itself as a fundamental-rights protection authority for high-risk systems in its letter to the Sejm Marshal on the bill - [prawo.pl / UODO materials](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/jak-pracodawcy-w-sektorze-publicznym-i-prywatnym-wdrazaja-ai-raport-uodo,1538683.html)

### Inferences
- An AI coding assistant used internally is normally a minimal-risk system: for the company the binding AI Act duties are Art. 4 literacy (now a softer "support" duty) and Art. 5. High-risk obligations become relevant only if the company builds/uses AI for e.g. recruitment or employee evaluation - and those are delayed to Dec 2027.
- A company that ships a product containing an LLM (chatbot for customers) must meet Art. 50 duties from 2 Aug 2026 - this is the most likely "live" obligation for a dev team in late 2026.
- Building a product on top of a third-party model and selling it under your brand can turn the company into a provider - a key point for developers.

### Gaps
- I did not re-read the consolidated Art. 4 / Art. 50 text post-Omnibus on EUR-Lex; exact wording should be checked.
- Exact Polish penalty amounts and KRiBSI's start of operations (likely tied to 28 Oct 2026 provisions) not confirmed from the statute text.
- Sources partly conflict on whether Art. 50 watermarking grace applies only to Art. 50(2) (Gibson Dunn) or more broadly.

## 2. GDPR / RODO with LLMs

### Takeaway
Any prompt containing personal data (customer records, logs with emails/IPs, HR data, names in code comments) is processing under GDPR: it needs a lawful basis, minimisation, a processor agreement (DPA, Art. 28) with the AI vendor, a transfer mechanism for non-EU vendors, and checking whether the vendor trains on inputs. EDPB Opinion 28/2024 and UODO 2026 materials make this explicit; UODO data shows most Polish organisations do not see the link.

### Cited Findings
- EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (18 Dec 2024, requested by the Irish DPA): AI model anonymity assessed case by case - "very unlikely" that individuals can be identified or data extracted by queries; legitimate interest possible via the three-step test (legitimate interest, strict necessity, balancing) with examples of conversational agents and cybersecurity; reasonable expectations criteria (public data, relationship, context); mitigating measures; if a model was developed with unlawfully processed data, this may affect the lawfulness of its deployment unless the model was anonymised - [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2024/edpb-opinion-ai-models-gdpr-principles-support-responsible-ai_en)
- GDPR principles relevant to prompts: Art. 5(1)(c) data minimisation, Art. 6 lawful basis, Art. 28 processor contract, Art. 35 DPIA for high-risk processing, Chapter V (Art. 44-49) international transfers (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- US transfers: EU-US Data Privacy Framework adequacy decision of 10 July 2023 covers certified US vendors; otherwise SCCs + transfer impact assessment (from background knowledge, verify current DPF litigation status) - [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection/eu-us-data-transfers_en)
- UODO advice for SMEs: before buying an AI tool define the problem, assess benefits/risks, establish whether employees will enter personal data of customers, employees, candidates or contractors; ensure a lawful basis and information obligations; sign a data processing agreement (umowa powierzenia) with the vendor; check whether the vendor uses data to train its models - [rp.pl on UODO](https://pro.rp.pl/abc-firmy/art45006251-kupujesz-narzedzie-ai-uodo-wskazuje-co-trzeba-zrobic-aby-korzystac-z-niego-zgodnie-z-prawem); [money.pl](https://www.money.pl/firma/firmy-masowo-siegaja-po-ai-wazny-urzad-ostrzega-przed-jednym-bledem-7319303924848832a.html)
- UODO (Aug 2026) published initial question lists on data protection when building/using AI (Społeczny Zespół Ekspertów), consultation until 30 Sep 2026; its needs survey found 41-58.5% of organisations do not see the link between AI tools and personal data processing, and 95.9% do not consider themselves ready to deploy AI in line with GDPR - [UODO file](https://uodo.gov.pl/pl/file/7856) (figures as reported in search summary, verify in document)
- UODO report on AI in employers: employees use ChatGPT, Copilot and other GenAI in daily office work, often entering personal data without realising the legal consequences - [prawo.pl](https://www.prawo.pl/biznes/jak-pracodawcy-w-sektorze-publicznym-i-prywatnym-wdrazaja-ai-raport-uodo,1538683.html)
- UODO President Mirosław Wróblewski asked the Minister of Labour for rules protecting candidates against discrimination by AI in recruitment - [wartowiedziec.pl](https://wartowiedziec.pl/serwis-glowny/aktualnosci/81310-sztuczna-inteligencja-decyduje-o-zatrudnieniu-pracownika-prezes-uodo-zwraca-uwage-na-potencjalne-zagrozenia)

### Inferences
- Practical rule for a junior dev: use only company-approved enterprise tiers (with DPA, no training on inputs, EU/DPF data handling); never paste production data, customer records, credentials or logs with personal data; use synthetic or anonymised data in prompts.
- A coding agent with access to a production database or tickets containing personal data is processing personal data even if the task is "just code".

### Gaps
- Did not find a dedicated UODO guideline for employees using ChatGPT; UODO materials are advisory/consultative.
- Current status of DPF challenges (Latombe case appeal) not verified in this session.

## 3. Intellectual property: copyright in AI-generated code, licence contamination, indemnities, trade secrets

### Takeaway
In Poland and the EU only a human can be an author; purely prompt-generated code is likely unprotected (effectively public domain), while code with sufficient creative human contribution (selection, modification, architecture) can be protected. Generated code can reproduce licensed open-source snippets; vendor indemnities exist but are conditional (business tiers, filters on, unmodified suggestions). Pasting company code into non-approved tools risks losing trade secret protection.

### Cited Findings
- Polish copyright law (ustawa o prawie autorskim i prawach pokrewnych): only a natural person can be the author (art. 8 as interpreted in literature), so AI output alone is not a work; content generated solely from a prompt as a rule is not protected; key criterion is human contribution and whether AI was only a tool; computer programs are protected works (art. 1 ust. 2 pkt 1) - [poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl](https://poradnikprzedsiebiorcy.pl/-chatgpt-a-prawa-autorskie-kto-jest-tworca-tresci-wygenerowanej-przez-ai); [PARP](https://www.parp.gov.pl/component/content/article/86364:wytwory-powstale-przy-udziale-ai-w-jaki-sposob-je-chronic)
- EU standard: a work must be the "author's own intellectual creation" (CJEU Infopaq, C-5/08); Software Directive 2009/24/EC Art. 1(3) applies the same originality test to programs (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2009/24/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/24/oj)
- Employee programs in Poland: economic rights to a program created by an employee within duties belong to the employer (art. 74 ust. 3 pr. aut.) (from background knowledge, verify) - [ISAP pr. aut.](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU19940240083)
- GitHub Copilot indemnity: Copilot Business/Enterprise customers are indemnified for unmodified suggestions only if the duplicate detection filter is set to "Block"; claims based on code differing from the suggestion are excluded; individual plans excluded; Microsoft notes duplicate detection does not currently apply to the Copilot coding agent - [GitHub community discussion](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/196578); [Devin blog quoting terms](https://devin.ai/blog/what-you-actually-get-and-dont-get-with-github-copilot-for-business); [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/azuredevcommunityblog/-/4468193)
- Licence contamination risk: tools can suggest code matching public repositories (copyleft obligations, attribution); scanners/filters are the recommended mitigation - [FOSSA](https://fossa.com/blog/5-ways-to-reduce-github-copilot-security-and-legal-risks/)
- Trade secrets: protection under Directive (EU) 2016/943 and Polish art. 11 ustawy o zwalczaniu nieuczciwej konkurencji requires the holder to take reasonable steps to keep information confidential (from background knowledge, verify) - [EUR-Lex 2016/943](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/943/oj)

### Inferences
- For companies, the main IP risk is not "who owns AI code" (employer contracts usually cover it) but (a) weaker ability to enforce copyright on mostly-generated code, and (b) incoming third-party licence obligations.
- Sending proprietary code to a consumer chatbot that may train on inputs can be argued to be a failure of "reasonable steps" - a company policy listing approved tools is both a GDPR and a trade-secret measure.
- Agent-mode tools (coding agents) may fall outside indemnity conditions designed for inline suggestions.

### Gaps
- No Polish or CJEU judgment specifically on AI-generated code found; CJEU pending case on GenAI and copyright (Like Company v Google, C-250/25) not verified this session.
- Indemnity terms of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google for business/API tiers not checked; verify current terms.
- No authoritative statistic on how often generated code reproduces licensed code.

## 4. Liability for errors from AI-generated code; Product Liability Directive; AI Liability Directive

### Takeaway
There is no special "AI made the bug" defence: the company placing software on the market is liable to customers, and under the new PLD (Directive (EU) 2024/2853) software including AI is a "product" with strict (no-fault) liability for products placed on the market after 9 Dec 2026. The AI Liability Directive was withdrawn in 2025. Internally, a Polish employee's liability for unintentional damage is capped by the Labour Code; contractors (B2B) bear contractual liability.

### Cited Findings
- PLD 2024/2853 extends "product" to software including AI systems (exception: non-commercial open-source software); applies to products placed on the market/put into service after 9 Dec 2026; older products stay under old regime unless substantially modified (e.g. extensive software upgrade) after that date - [Buse](https://buse.de/en/blog-en/commercial-en/what-will-change-with-the-new-eu-product-liability-directive-eu-2024-2853/); [xictron](https://www.xictron.com/en/blog/new-product-liability-law-software-december-2026/); text: [EUR-Lex 2024/2853](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)
- PLD claimant aids: disclosure of evidence (courts can order disclosure, potentially incl. technical documentation), rebuttable presumptions of defect and causation where technical complexity makes proof excessively difficult; missing security updates can make a product defective - [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://connections.nortonrosefulbright.com/post/102jpjp/revised-product-liability-directive-introducing-rules-on-strict-liability-for-ai); [Wolf Theiss](https://www.wolftheiss.com/insights/software-digital-products-and-ai-stricter-safety-requirements-on-the-horizon/)
- AI Liability Directive: listed for withdrawal in Commission 2025 Work Programme (11 Feb 2025) due to no foreseeable agreement; Commission reserved the option of a new proposal - [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/proposed-eu-ai-liability-rules-withdrawn); [TCI Law](https://www.tcilaw.de/en/ai-liability-eu-commission-withdraws-proposal-for-directive/); [Verfassungsblog](https://verfassungsblog.de/anatomy-of-a-fall-aiact-aild-pld/)
- Polish employee liability: an employee who causes damage through non-intentional breach of duties is liable up to 3 times monthly remuneration (art. 119 Kodeks pracy); full liability for intentional damage (art. 122) (from background knowledge, verify) - [ISAP Kodeks pracy](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU19740240141)

### Inferences
- Accountability stays with humans and the company: the developer who merges and the reviewer who approves AI code own it professionally; legally the company answers to clients (contract, PLD), and recourse against the AI vendor is limited by its terms.
- PLD makes poor security practices (unpatched dependencies, unreviewed generated code) a direct strict-liability exposure from Dec 2026 - a strong argument for code review, tests and SBOMs.

### Gaps
- Polish PLD transposition bill status (deadline 9 Dec 2026) not found.
- Typical AI vendor liability caps in enterprise terms not checked.

## 5. Organisational consequences: quality, technical debt, productivity, deskilling, cost, accountability

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed: adoption is near-universal and throughput can rise, but large-scale code analyses show more duplication and less refactoring, DORA finds AI amplifies existing strengths and weaknesses (stability often suffers), and the only RCT on experienced developers found a 19% slowdown with early-2025 tools while developers believed they were 20% faster; METR's 2026 follow-up suggests some speedup but METR itself calls it unreliable.

### Cited Findings
**Productivity - METR**
- METR RCT (published 10 July 2025): 16 experienced OSS developers, 246 real issues (~2h each) in repos they knew (avg. ~5 years); with AI allowed tasks took 19% longer (95% CI +2% to +39%); developers predicted 24% speedup and afterwards still believed in 20% speedup; tools mainly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet; METR now marks the results as out of date - [METR 2025](https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/)
- METR update (24 Feb 2026, late-2025 tools): returning developers -18% time (i.e. speedup, CI -38% to +9%), new developers -4% (CI -15% to +9%); METR considers this unreliable because developers refused to work without AI (30-50% withheld tasks), pay was cut from USD 150/h to 50/h, and time tracking fails with concurrent agents; estimates likely lower bounds of the speedup; study is being redesigned - [METR 2026](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)

**DORA (Google)**
- DORA 2024: a 25% increase in AI adoption was associated with an estimated 1.5% decrease in delivery throughput and 7.2% decrease in delivery stability (from background knowledge, verify) - [DORA 2024](https://dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/)
- DORA 2025 "State of AI-assisted Software Development": ~5,000 respondents, 90% use AI (+14 pp YoY); AI now associated with higher throughput but continued instability problems; AI is an "amplifier" of strengths of high performers and dysfunctions of struggling teams - [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/dora-state-of-ai-in-dev-2025); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-dora-ai-report-2025/); primary: [dora.dev](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/)
- Analysis of DORA 2025 team clusters: for most clusters (1-4) speed gains are cancelled by rising change failure rate; only top clusters gain throughput without harming stability - [Rob Bowley](https://blog.robbowley.net/2025/10/01/dora-2025-ai-assisted-dev-report-some-benefit-most-dont/) (opinion/analysis)

**Code quality - GitClear**
- GitClear "AI Copilot Code Quality 2025": 211m changed lines 2020-2024; ~8x increase in duplicated code blocks (5+ lines) in 2024; 2024 first year copy/pasted lines exceeded moved (refactored) lines; moved lines down ~39.9%; attributed to tab-to-accept insertion and limited context - [devclass](https://devclass.com/2025/02/20/ai-is-eroding-code-quality-states-new-in-depth-report/); [LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/software-quality/how-ai-generated-code-accelerates-technical-debt); primary: [GitClear](https://www.gitclear.com/ai_assistant_code_quality_2025_research)
- Caveat: GitClear sells code-analytics tools; secondary sources give inconsistent baselines (e.g. moved lines 15.88% to 3.10% vs -39.9%) - [Substack summary](https://waynesfuture.substack.com/p/ai-and-code-quality-faster-more-churn)

**Deskilling / juniors**
- Anthropic RCT (Jan 2026, "How AI assistance impacts the formation of coding skills"): developers learning a new Python library with AI assistance scored notably lower on a follow-up comprehension quiz (reported ~17 pp, about 50% vs 67%) without significant time savings; outcome depended on how AI was used (asking for explanations vs delegating) (from background knowledge, verify) - [Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)

### Inferences
- Credible position for a junior: AI speeds up some work but is not a guaranteed productivity multiplier; perceived speed is not measured speed; quality must be protected by review, tests, refactoring discipline and DORA-style metrics (change failure rate).
- Juniors face a real learning risk if they delegate understanding; using AI to explain and then writing/verifying code themselves preserves skill-building.
- Accountability: the human who commits/approves owns the code; teams should mark AI-assisted changes and keep review standards unchanged.

### Gaps
- No reliable, sourced cost figures (licence cost per seat, token spend of agents) gathered in this session.
- Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey trust figures (more developers distrust AI accuracy than trust it) not verified this session - check [survey.stackoverflow.co/2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/).
- No Poland-specific data on AI coding adoption or quality found.
